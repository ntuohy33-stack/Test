Baptism of a Child

Thank you for bringing your child to be baptised. It is one of the best things we do.

When we baptise a child, we are saying something about God before we say anything about the child: that God's love arrives first, before any of us have done a thing to deserve it. Your child will not remember the day. That is rather the point.

Baptism is also a promise the whole congregation makes — to help your child grow up knowing they are loved by God and belong here. You will hear us make it out loud.

The one thing we ask of you

A parent declares their own faith

During the service, at least one parent or guardian is asked to declare their own Christian faith. It is three short questions: do you repent of your sins, do you turn to Christ, do you commit yourself to God. You answer in a sentence each.

For those unsure about making this declaration, we can also offer a service of Thanksgiving for the Birth of a Child.

If your child is older

Some children are old enough to want to answer for themselves, and there is no minimum age in our church. Where that is the case, Church Council decides — on the minister's advice — whether your child comes as an adult candidate answering the questions themselves, or as a child candidate with you carrying the responsibility. Both are proper. If your child is answering for themselves, use the form Taking the Step instead.

What happens next

  1. 1A conversation — at church or at your kitchen table, whichever suits.
  2. 2Church Council is asked to approve, which is ordinary and straightforward.
  3. 3We settle on a Sunday that works for your family and anyone travelling.
  4. 4Baptism happens in the middle of the normal service, with everybody there.
