Membership & discipleship

On the Way with Jesus

A new generation finding Jesus and his people in the Adelaide Hills and beyond.

Whether you have been part of this church for a very long time or arrived only recently, there is a next step to take. The next step is not paperwork; it is a cup of tea or a coffee and an unhurried conversation about where you have come from and where you are.

Where you might be now

Which of these sounds like you?

I’ve been coming for a while

You are fully welcome here. But we would like to ask you, gently: is there a next step you have been wondering about? Sometimes the answer is a good one. Sometimes it is just that nobody ever asked.

I was baptised as a child

You may have assumed you were a member all along — many have. Christ’s claim on you was laid down at the font and has never been withdrawn. What is on offer is the chance to answer it out loud. Come and have a conversation; there is no hurry and no embarrassment.

We’d like our child baptised

When we baptise a child, we are saying something about God before we say anything about the child: that God’s love arrives first, before any of us have done a thing to deserve it. Your child will not remember the day. That is rather the point.

The first step is a conversation — at church or at your kitchen table, whichever suits.

I’ve never been baptised

Then the invitation is to the beginning: to be claimed, named and welcomed. There is no minimum age and no waiting list. There is a conversation, and then a Sunday.

If you would also like to become a member of the church, both things happen together in the same service.

Grace goes first

For by grace you have been saved through faith, and this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God — not the result of works, so that no one may boast.
Ephesians 2:8–9

Everything here rests on something that was true before you thought about it. Nothing is a bar to clear or a standard to meet. God acts first, and we respond.

What happens next

The way it works

  1. A conversationWe sit down over a cuppa and talk it through properly. No rush.
  2. Church CouncilChurch Council is asked to approve. This is a warm formality, not an examination.
  3. A Sunday that suitsWe pick a Sunday that suits you and the people you want there.
  4. Your promisesOn the day you make your promises, you are signed with the cross, and hands are laid on you while we pray for you.

For a child’s baptism, it happens in the middle of the normal service, with everybody there.

Our common life

Four questions, one hope

When someone is confirmed in this church, four questions are asked. They are not our invention, and they are not a high bar erected by the keen. They are what the whole church, everywhere, asks of everyone who says yes. Not four separate programs, but one hope worked out four ways: a new generation finding Jesus and his people in the Adelaide Hills and beyond.

Worship

Will you continue in the community of faith, the apostles’ teaching, the breaking of bread and the prayers?

We will gather for worship, read the Scriptures, come to the Table, and pray — together and alone.

Witness

Will you proclaim, by word and example, the good news of God in Christ?

We will proclaim the good news of God in Christ by word and example, in the places we already are.

Welcome

Will you seek Christ in all people, and love your neighbour as yourself?

We will offer our gifts, our time and our money in service, and seek Christ in every person we meet.

Wholeness

Will you strive for justice and peace and respect the dignity of every human being?

We will strive for justice and peace, and respect the dignity of every human being.

Nobody is asked to be certain about everything, or to have their life sorted out.

What membership means here

Four rolls, all of them belonging

The Uniting Church keeps four rolls, and every one of them describes people who genuinely belong.

Baptised members

Everyone baptised who has not yet been confirmed. This includes our children, and it includes adults baptised long ago who have never made their own profession of faith. If that is you, you are already a member of Christ’s church. What is missing is your yes, and confirmation is where it is given.

Confirmed members

Those who, being baptised, have affirmed the faith for themselves. The Constitution puts it plainly: a confirmed member is one “recognised as having affirmed the faith, and as sharing the right and responsibility of participation in the work and government of the Church.”

Members-in-association

Those who belong to another denomination, or hold membership in another Uniting Church congregation, while sharing fully in the life of this one. If you are Anglican or Catholic or Baptist by background and Echunga is now your church, this is very likely you. It is recognised by Church Council on application, and it is a real membership, not a consolation prize.

Adherents

Those who worship with us and share our life without being members. You are counted, you are welcome, you may serve on committees and speak at congregational meetings. Under the Regulations, adherents do not vote in elections — which is worth saying plainly now rather than discovering at a meeting.

Two honest notes. The first is that confirmed members and members-in-association carry responsibility as well as rights: they are the ones who elect elders and councillors, and only they can be elected. If you care how this church is led, that matters. The second is that none of this measures anyone’s standing with God. A roll is a record of who is travelling with us, kept so that we know who we are and can care for one another properly. It is not a scorecard.
